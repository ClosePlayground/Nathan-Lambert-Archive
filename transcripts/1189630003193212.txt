Hi, there's been some renewed discussion about development at Preston Market, so I thought
I'd just recap where things were up to. As most people probably know, there are multiple
landlords across the Preston Market site, but the majority of the properties were
purchased by the Medich family back in 2004 in a joint venture with the Tarasco family.
And pretty much for the last 20 years, that joint venture has continuously sought
planning permission for two controversial propositions. The first was to move most
of the traders out of the existing buildings and into a new double-storey building built
here on the Aldi car park site so that it would have faced Cramer Street. Once it was
completed, the existing buildings on the western side were going to be demolished.
Suffice to say that was not popular with most people in the community. It was temporarily
blocked by Darebin Council on a number of occasions. And then it was permanently
blocked by our state government in 2023 when we put robust, proper heritage
protections on the existing buildings, ensuring that they had to be kept and taking
that particular option off the table. But the second controversial proposal was to
build apartments over the car parks, especially here on the western side, close
to Preston train station. If we're honest, that was also not popular with a lot of
people in the community. But in contrast, it was mostly supported by Darebin
councillors of all political persuasions, green labor and independents.
And in fact, at one point, the majority owners actually had approved building
permits for a 14-storey building up on Murray Road, which I understand was
going to have a Coles under it. But in essence, every political party,
every independent, every community group, including the Save the Preston
Market Group, all said that they were OK with at least 800 apartments and a story
height of at least 10 storeys in places. And that is because it's good
public policy. Building more homes close to public transport is a big,
important urban project for Melbourne. It's good for congestion.
It's good for emissions. It's good for housing affordability.
Obviously, the key question here, the specific question is whether it is also
in this case good for the Preston Market and good for the local area.
And probably that really depends a lot on the more detailed components around
open space, canopy cover, infrastructure contributions, pedestrian access,
bike access, all those things that we will debate if and when we see a
proposal. Finally, there is a bit of discussion about public acquisition.
Darribin Council are doing a feasibility study into that at the moment.
Again, it sort of depends on the apartment question.
Public acquisition for the purpose of building apartments on the site is
obviously a very different proposition to public acquisition for the purpose
of blocking apartments on the site. We will see what the Darribin
Council study has to say about those two possibilities.
We do expect at some point for the majority owners to put forward a proposal.
There is a rumour it might be sometime soon.
We guess it might look something like the Darribin Council vision from 2022,
at least in terms of its scale.
But whatever it looks like, there will then be a long period of planning
consideration, planning permits, building permits for every stage that
will spend many years, that will probably spend many elections.
Nothing will happen particularly quickly.
And there will be a lot of opportunity for public debate,
including amongst people who shop here all the time and love it,
including amongst the traders, of course, who are the heart of the market.
All of those people have the opportunity to put their views forward.
And the Minister, in so far as she makes any decisions,
will take all of that into account.
