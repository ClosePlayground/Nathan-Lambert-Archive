Acting Speaker, on 6 October the AFL announced that they were evoking the VFL licence of
the Preston Bulance and I believe they did so in a way that sought to maximise the
chances that a club that has been part of the Preston community for 143 years would
no longer exist. Acting Speaker, I don't understand what the AFL are doing with
the standalone VFL clubs. As people observe, there are effectively three types of clubs.
There are AFL seconds teams which are heavily cross-subsidised by their parent
there are clubs that are heavily cross-subsidised by their pokey operations and then there are
the other standalone clubs which are at a permanent financial disadvantage and some
cynics certainly suggest that as those clubs inevitably go through their ups and downs
and finish on the bottom of the ladder as one of them usually does the AFL will one by one
knock them off and as we said to the AFL during conversations about the club's future in
2023 it seems to us at least that they need to make one of two decisions either they are going
to permanently subsidise those standalone VFL clubs to recognise their role in the history
and diversity of the game or else they need to re-establish a statewide Victorian competition
which would have lower costs and a level playing field and so we can talk about the specifics
of the Bulance and we will do so but most importantly first and foremost we call upon the
AFL again to make one of those two decisions in order to give those traditional
standalone clubs a secure long-term future.
