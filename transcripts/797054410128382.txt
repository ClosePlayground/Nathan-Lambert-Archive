Hi everyone, just an update on some industrial relations matters. Obviously the big news
this week was that government school staff stopped work on Tuesday with respect to the
VGSA agreement. The headline figures there are that the offer on the table is approximately
18 per cent over four years and the unions involved in the negotiations are seeking
approximately 35 per cent. The AEU, who are the main union on that agreement, are
also currently negotiating the Vectea and Triple EA agreements for early childhood professionals.
The headline offer there is 39.6 per cent over four years for educators and to match
school teacher pay sky levels for early childhood teachers. And then locally there
is also the possibility that Darebin Council staff and workers may take protected industrial
action soon. I believe they are seeking 22 per cent over four years. I'm not sure
if that had an offer back. People watching television may have also noticed ABC workers
are on strike at the moment or certainly were yesterday. But one important thing
to say about all of these public sector agreements is that while the headline figures
are crucial, there is a lot of detail in them. They normally run to over 100
and traditionally public sector agreements have been a chance to push forward some important
progressive reforms, things like reproductive leave, gender affirmation leave, work from home
arrangements, other flexible work arrangements. And of course, they've also been a chance to
deal with some of the inequality that deals, that appears within the pay scales of the
agreement. So often the average pay scale in a public sector agreement will be around
$120,000 a year, but there will be some people on easily more than double that,
some people on less than half that, and these discussions and negotiations are a chance to
reduce that gap. AU members in particular will be aware that the union settled the TAFE
agreement last year for I think 21 per cent over four years with some important
changes to workloads and that disparity of classifications, and we do hope to see
similar good outcomes with respect to this year's agreements. I won't try and run through
every other agreement that the Victorian Government is currently involved in because
there's always a lot, but I just want to recognise local UFU firefighters who make a
big contribution to our community, both professionally and personally, and who have
now been involved in unresolved negotiations regarding their agreements since July of 2020.
Obviously, if you're a union member, you should be well informed about where your own
agreements are up to, and of course you do have the right to take protected industrial
action as legalised by the Keating Labor Government and the right to negotiate across
multiple employers as Darrabin are doing as legalised by the Albanese Labor Government,
but we are very happy to answer any general questions people might have or to pass on
feedback to the relevant portfolios, ministers or public sector organisations involved in these discussions.
