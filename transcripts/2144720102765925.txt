Some of you might have seen that on Saturday a group of Preston line supporters put up
some very racist and misogynist banners that were directed towards Greek Victorians and
Greek Victorian women in particular. Those banners were taken down quickly by the security
at the ground but photos of them then circulated on Instagram and other social media platforms
apparently through some of these so-called ultras accounts. We understand that as of
today the people involved in this behaviour have been identified and we are expecting
the club to put out a statement shortly setting out the very serious consequences
that they will face and then Football Victoria will announce further disciplinary action
we expect at some point next week. And of course as a state government we introduced
new laws last year, strong laws that made it easier to prosecute racial or gender
based vilification including in serious cases as a criminal offence and including in very
serious cases with potential jail terms. But I think it's also important with this
particular incident to mention the additional context anyone who grew up playing soccer
in the 80s or 90s will know that certain intra-European rivalries have been part of
the game in this state for a very long time and I think sometimes we can make the mistake
of romanticising that sort of stuff, I think I've been guilty of that, seeing
it as some sort of authentic working class multiculturalism. It's not that and surely
this incident reminds us how much it's not that. The clubs might be that but
the conflict is not. It has always been ugly, it has often been explicitly racist,
it has often been very personal and unfortunately and as was the case on Saturday it's
often involved kids. I remember seeing Serbian and Croatian under 12s or under 14s
maybe saying awful things to each other because they were being egged on by older
members of their clubs and so I think it's important to recognise that we have had
behaviour occur here that has caused people to feel objectified, it has caused them
to feel racially vilified, none of that is acceptable but it has also separately
and additionally caused them to feel unsafe because people are deliberately escalating
this perceived nationalist conflict and that is something that nobody wants. So hopefully
the club and particularly Football Victoria will address that as well through their
statements and through their actions. As a state government we'll continue our work
to tackle vilification and racial hatred and gender based hatred but also our important
reforms to unmask anonymous accounts on the internet which have played a role here
as well and hopefully between all those things we can finally get to the point
where we can talk about soccer, enjoy soccer, enjoy last minute upset wins and all the
great things about the game without also having to talk about this abhorrent behaviour
that has been part of the game locally in Victoria for way too long.
