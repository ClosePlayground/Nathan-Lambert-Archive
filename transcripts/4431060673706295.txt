My adjournment matter concerns the controversial local issue of the Merrilands single dwelling
covenant. I actually addressed this issue earlier this week, Speaker, but I would like
to take the opportunity this evening to make a formal adjournment action request of the
Minister for Planning. I will come to the exact details of that action request shortly.
By way of background, the Merrilands covenant is a private legal agreement that has been
in place since that area was subdivided, mostly following World War II. In essence,
it prevents most landowners north of Broadhurst in that part of the reservoir from subdividing
their property into townhouses or apartments. I should say, Speaker, it is a very popular
policy in that part of the world. Many people moved there specifically because
of the existence of the covenant and, of course, made a very large financial decision,
a very big life decision, to move into that part of the world.
But clearly, also, Speaker, it is an arrangement that is at odds with our government's
housing policy. We are not seeking to stop townhouses or apartments and, in fact, on
the contrary, we are seeking to build more townhouses and more apartments particularly
close to transport hubs and particularly close to train stations in order to reduce
emissions, to deal with congestion, to improve housing affordability, but most importantly
to give people, and particularly young people, the opportunity to live close to jobs, to
education and the cultural or social opportunities that they would like.
So that is an important policy to us and so we have reformed the Planning and Environment
Act to make it easier to remove the restrictive covenant.
However, there does remain, Speaker, a critical question in this particular policy
area, and that is whether those applications to remove the covenant will be Type 2 or
Type 3 applications. I would just like to put on the record my very strong support
for the idea that they should be Type 3 applications and, in fact, my adjournment
action for the Minister for Planning is to make any application to remove or vary
the covenant a Type 3 application. And I think, Speaker, that that is consistent
with the way we have approached our planning reforms generally. Of course, we are encouraging
making it easier, encouraging people to build homes, making it easier to build homes right
across Victoria, but of course we are doing so in a way that reflects the existing
planning structure that has been built up over a long time and has set planning
expectations over a long time, and I think it makes sense that in doing so we acknowledge
that restrictive covenants, whilst an unusual part of the system, are nonetheless a legitimate
part of the system and should be treated that way. And I note in closing, Speaker,
that we have a clear position on this, but the Greens can date, the Liberal can
date, do not.
