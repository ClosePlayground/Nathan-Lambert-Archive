Hi. We've just had a briefing this afternoon from the majority owners of Preston Market
on their draft development plans. We'll put some more detail in the comments, but just
wanted to make some quick, broad points. Firstly and most importantly, the draft plans,
including all of the technical reports, will be made available to the public probably
later this week, so everyone will have a chance to see them for themselves. They
are, broadly speaking, what we expected. They align with what the state government
set out in our planning amendments. They align with what Darebin Council unanimously
endorsed back in 2023. The current market is retained, except for the South Shed
and the Aldi component. That is totally rebuilt with a new larger supermarket, a more
sort of Coles Woolworth-sized supermarket facing onto Cramer Street. That part's actually
the first stage of the whole project, so that will be important. And then the market
itself is extended mainly west, so mainly towards the train station to compensate
for that loss of the South Shed. And then, of course, there are the controversial
changes to the areas that are currently open carparks. There will be some new green
open space, including a new park on Murray Road, which will be great. But of course,
it's mostly new pathways and buildings. Those buildings have retail on the
ground floor, then podium carparks above that, and then either apartments above
that or elevated open green space. Parts of it look a bit like the
Highline in New York. For people who know that thing, we'll see how that
goes through the planning stages. But very surprisingly, the total number of
apartments is roughly 900, which is a lot, but which is actually less than
what the owners were allowed to do under the planning amendment. So while it
goes up to 16 stories up on Murray Road, right up there, the buildings are
actually a lot thinner than we expected. It's a lot less bulky. There's
a lot of space in between. And as we've said before, apartments near train
stations like Preston Station are something we support as a government.
They are good for congestion. They are good for emissions. They are good
for housing affordability. And those, of course, are the same reasons why
every political party in Darrabin supports them. That's why Darrabin
Council has always unanimously supported them. Now, obviously, there are
differences of opinion between the different models. You have the
Victorian Socialist model, where you publicly acquire the entire site.
You build a lot of public housing around it. Ours, of course, it
mainly remains a private site. It's always been a private site,
except for the St George's Murray Road corner. But as I say, if you
set aside those questions of tenure, every political party and
every independent for the last 20 years has supported apartments between
the market and the train station that look like the apartments in
this plan. And then finally, the whole thing will be staged out over
a very long period between roughly 2030 and 2040. So this is a very early
stage, a very early draft. But one of the key early priorities is
upgrading the sewerage water and other utility systems that are
really struggling at the moment. I imagine traders will be quite
supportive in those early stages. I understand they have also been
briefed, but nobody has yet seen the full draft plans with all the
technical reports. They'll be available for everyone to see later
this week and then everyone can provide their feedback.
