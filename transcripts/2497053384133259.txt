Hi, we're back in Keon Park and I just wanted to summarise where things are at with the
61 Johnson Street social housing development. The first important point to make of course
is that as a Labor Government we absolutely support social housing and we support the
specific model that is being used here, where our colleagues in the Federal Labor
Government are providing the funding, the subsidy to make this important project happen.
We are not going to do that thing that sometimes other political parties and other
politicians do, including sometimes the Greens, where they say they support new
social housing in theory. Everyone always says they support it in theory and then
people find an excuse to oppose specific projects when they happen in the real world.
We're not doing that. We support this specific project and we're certainly not
going to say no to a Federal Government subsidy of over $20,000 per apartment
per year that will make a real difference for local people in need.
However, a second important thing also to say is that we absolutely believe and
agree that any form of Government supported housing must of course be safe
for residents and safe for other community members. Now 99% of the time it is.
There are roughly 4,000 people who live in public housing or community
housing across Preston and Reservoir. And as we all know, the vast, vast majority
of those people are fantastic, wonderful people who make a really positive
contribution to the economy, to the community, I should say, and the economy
as well. But at any given point in time, in our experience, there will be
a handful, maybe 10 or 20 across all the different locations, half a percent
maybe, who are causing some issues, sometimes serious issues for other
residents and neighbours. It does happen. It happens in private housing
as well, of course. But wherever it happens, you do need landlords, in this
case social housing teams, who are prepared to act quickly to provide
people with the support they need and sometimes to make the tough decisions
to issue compliance orders or even transfer people out of a specific
building with the appropriate supports. And again, this is a point of
difference between the political parties, but it is something we believe
in very strongly. It is possible, absolutely possible, to have perfectly
safe community housing as most community housing is, as long as you
are a bit proactive about just ensuring that people are matched with
the level of support that they need. And a consequence of that, an
unavoidable consequence of that triaged or streamed arrangement, is that
the very, very small percentage of people who behave in a repeatedly
anti-social manner or even in an outright illegal manner, will have
and should have a smaller set of options available to them, which
will not include developments like this one, like this particular
development, that if you look at their funding model, this one is
funded at the low support need end of the spectrum. Now we've made
that point to ministers on a number of occasions and will continue
to do so. And then finally, the other major issue people have is
parking. Darabin Council are currently undertaking a review of
parking in the Johnson Street area, but unfortunately, it will
not be ready in time to provide input into this project. We
have said to the relevant ministers, we don't think that
Keon Park is a walkable neighbourhood in the way that the
project claims it is. We think people probably will have and
need cars. And so the project will need more parking, just
like the Trinity and Capella apartments next door. People
have raised a lot of other issues as well. We will provide more
details of that in the comments, as well as details of the
next steps. But we just want to cover off on those three key
issues of parking safety and our overall support for this
important social housing project right here in Keon Park.
