Speaker, last week I had the opportunity to catch Reservoir High's great production
of The Day the Internet Died, a very topical satire about the impact of social media and
smartphone technologies on local communities. I would like to congratulate Directors Tegan
French and Ashley Tucker and the whole cast and crew, and particularly Year 12's Indie
Quinn and Rick Nevros, who have been part of every school production every year of
their time at the school.
Speaker, I would also like to thank Jodie Purchase and Daryl Forbes for hosting
a very enthusiastic audience of students from Preston North East Primary, Kingsbury
Primary and Reservoir Views Primary, and some equally enthusiastic residents from Latrobe
Retirement Village.
But, Speaker, when you watch our school productions at different schools, you do
notice that they appear to be funded in different ways. Any information the Minister
can provide about how our government supports creative productions in our fantastic public
schools would be greatly appreciated.
