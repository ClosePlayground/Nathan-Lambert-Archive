Hi everyone, I just want to provide an update on the effects of new planning laws on the
Merrilands Restrictive Covenant. These laws technically haven't passed the Parliament
yet but they have been approved by Labor and Green MPs in the Upper House and I have
no doubt that they will pass in the new year. And in essence the changes give Darebin
Council or VCAT the discretion to remove the Covenant through the normal planning
permit process. And I think it is likely that either Council or VCAT will do that
for a significant number of subdivisions and multi dwelling developments in this area.
So it is a big change. Now the Covenant will continue to have an effect and probably
the best way to explain it is that if you go back five years it used to often be
the case that someone trying to subdivide in any general residential zone often faced
a long battle that ended up in VCAT but then they would win in VCAT anyway unless
they were doing something unusual. With our housing statement reforms we have dramatically
sped up that process for building new homes because you can no longer appeal a
development to VCAT unless it is doing something unusual. However all of those
old requirements around notice to neighbors, objections, Council
consideration, VCAT consideration all that will continue to apply for
properties covered by restrictive Covenants. So in effect it's become
easier to build housing in general residential zones but now restrictive
Covenant areas have become like the old general residential zones and I
suspect that will mean that larger developers don't look to Marylands
because they will want to go and build somewhere where their pathway to
development is faster. But for people who own land in Marylands and want to
subdivide it no doubt some of them will try to do that as soon as the
new laws come into effect in 2027 and I imagine they will have a
reasonable success rate in removing the Covenant particularly in those
areas marked in Darraben Council's housing strategy in orange that are
marked for increased change along High Street, Marnies Road, Radford Road
and the areas near the train station I think it's very likely that
applications to remove the Covenant to remove the Covenant will be successful
there. So none of this is legal advice and as many people know restrictive
Covenants are complex instruments and we're still trying to understand how
Council decisions and VCAT decisions will affect the Marylands one but I
just wanted to convey the general gist of the changes because I know the
Covenant is a significant topic of discussion in the community it's very
popular among a lot of people and I think it's just important to be really
clear that these changes will reduce the strength of the Covenant and lead
to more multi-dwelling development in this area in the long run.
